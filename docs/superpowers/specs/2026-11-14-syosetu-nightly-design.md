# Syosetu nightly check and fetch

Date: 2026-11-14. Replaces the TODO entries "Syosetu: periodic background fetch" and "Syosetu: daily update check".

## Why

A syosetu series gets new chapters only when its owner opens it and runs a fetch, and a new series stops at chapter 1.
`series.syosetu_updated_at` (the Library's "Latest syosetu update" sort) is refreshed only by that fetch or by opening
the chapter list, so the sort is as stale as the owner's last visit. Once a night, the server should refresh every
followed series' date and download the chapters it does not have yet.

## Goals

1. Once a day, at a time the admin sets, every eligible series has its syosetu index read and `syosetu_updated_at`
   refreshed.
2. In the same pass, an eligible series with chapters numbered above its *mark* (below) gets a `syosetu_fetch` job for
   exactly those chapters. A series created after this ships (chapter 1 only) therefore gets its whole backlog on the
   first night.
3. A failed check is visible on the series, and a series can be switched out of the nightly pass.

## Non-goals

- No gap filling. Chapters at or below the series' mark (deleted on purpose, including the newest ones, or a chapter that
  failed inside a fetch) are never fetched by the nightly pass; the series page's manual fetch stays the way to get them.
  (User's choice: re-downloading deleted chapters every night would be wrong; a user who deleted by mistake fetches by
  hand.)
- No backlog for series that exist when this ships. Their first nightly check sets the mark at syosetu's latest chapter
  and fetches nothing; only chapters posted after that arrive by themselves. (User's choice, to keep the first night
  after deploy from downloading for hours; the manual fetch covers a backlog they want.)
- No per-user schedule. One server-wide time; every user's syosetu requests share the process-wide throttle anyway.
- No notifications (email, push, header badge). New chapters simply appear; the Jobs page shows what happened.
- No re-fetch of chapters the series already has (no detection of edited chapters on syosetu).
- No change to the fetch job's behaviour or to volume grouping.

## Anti-goals

- A History row per series per night when nothing was posted.
- The tick blocking for minutes (it also starts the Ollama night runs).
- Hammering syosetu: every request still goes through `scrape.throttle` and `syosetu_delay_seconds`.

## Approaches considered

- **A. The tick queues one `syosetu_nightly` job per user; that job reads the indexes and queues ordinary
  `syosetu_fetch` jobs. (Chosen.)** One History row per user per night plus one per series that got chapters; the fetch
  job is reused unchanged; the worker's restore pause already covers it.
- B. The tick queues a `syosetu_fetch` per eligible series with a "whatever is new" mode. Less code, but a History row per
  series every night and the date check tied to fetching. Rejected.
- C. The tick reads the indexes itself. At 5 s a series, 40 series block the tick for minutes and delay the Ollama night
  runs. Rejected.

## Design

### Eligible series

A series is eligible when all hold:

- `syosetu_url` is non-blank (not `source`, which the Settings tab can edit freely);
- `status = 'ongoing'`;
- `archived_at IS NULL`;
- the new flag `syosetu_nightly` is true.

A short story (one chapter, chapter URL = series URL) is eligible but never yields anything new; no special case.

### New columns on `series` (one autogenerated migration)

| Column | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `syosetu_nightly` | boolean not null | true | in the nightly pass |
| `syosetu_fetched_through` | integer null | existing rows NULL; new rows 0 | the mark (below) |
| `syosetu_checked_at` | timestamptz null | NULL | last successful index read |
| `syosetu_check_error` | text not null | `''` | last nightly failure message; `''` = none |

Existing series get `syosetu_nightly = true` (server default), so they join the pass on the first night after deploy.
`syosetu_fetched_through` has a Python-side `default=0` and **no** server default, so the migration leaves existing rows
NULL and every series created afterwards starts at 0.

### The mark

`syosetu_fetched_through` is the highest syosetu chapter number the series has taken, so that deleting chapters (even the
newest) never makes them come back.

- **New chapters** for the nightly pass = index chapters with `num > max(mark, highest series_order)`. The `series_order`
  term covers chapters that arrived without a fetch (a ta.db import, a URL added later in Settings).
- **The fetch job** (any `syosetu_fetch`, by hand or nightly) raises a non-NULL mark to the highest chapter number it
  added: `mark = max(mark, max(added nums))`. It never touches a NULL mark.
- **NULL = not seeded yet** (series that existed when this shipped). The first successful nightly index read sets
  `mark = max(highest index num, highest series_order)` and queues nothing for that series. It is not seeded by manual
  reads or fetches, so a hand fetch of chapter 15 of 800 before the first night cannot turn into a 785-chapter download.
- An index whose highest number is below the mark (syosetu removed chapters) lowers nothing; the mark only rises.
- The Settings tab may show it read-only ("Nightly fetch takes chapters after N"); not editable.

`syosetu_checked_at` is set, and `syosetu_check_error` cleared, by **every** successful index read: the nightly job,
the fetch job's index read (`api/fetch_job.py`) and `_read_index` in `api/routers/scrape.py`. So a failure note does not
linger after the owner fetched by hand. Only the nightly job writes `syosetu_check_error`.

`SeriesUpdate` accepts `syosetu_nightly`; `SeriesOut` returns all three.

### Server settings

Three new keys in `api/server_settings.py` (DB row > env > default, like the others), with matching fields in
`api/config.py`:

| Key | Env | Default | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `syosetu_nightly_enabled` | `TA_SYOSETU_NIGHTLY_ENABLED` | `true` | bool |
| `syosetu_nightly_time` | `TA_SYOSETU_NIGHTLY_TIME` | `"04:00"` | `HH:MM`, 24-hour |
| `syosetu_nightly_tz` | `TA_SYOSETU_NIGHTLY_TZ` | `"UTC"` | an IANA zone `ZoneInfo` accepts |

Settings → Admin → Server (`web/src/admin/ServerSettingsForm.tsx`) shows them: a checkbox, a `<input type="time">`, and
the zone (a text field prefilled from the browser's `Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone` when the admin
first changes the time; the builder may choose a `<select>` instead).

### The tick

The once-a-minute loop moves out of `api/ollama/schedule.py` into `api/ticker.py`, which calls, each in its own session
and its own `try` (one failing must not skip the other), inside `state.work()` as today:

1. `api.ollama.schedule.tick(db, now)` — unchanged;
2. `api.syosetu_nightly.tick(db, now)` — new.

`api/main.py` starts `ticker.ticker(stop)` instead of `schedule.ticker(stop)`. Tests keep calling `tick` functions
directly.

`syosetu_nightly.tick(db, now)`:

- Reads `server_settings.effective(db)`. Does nothing when disabled.
- `day` = `now` in `syosetu_nightly_tz`, as a date; does nothing while the local time is before `syosetu_nightly_time`.
- For every user who owns at least one eligible series and has no `syosetu_nightly` job with
  `params_json["day"] == day.isoformat()` (any state), enqueue `syosetu_nightly` with `{"day": "<YYYY-MM-DD>"}`.
- Commits its own session (a sanctioned commit outside the route boundary, like the Ollama tick; add it to the list in
  `CLAUDE.md`).

Consequences, all intended: a server that was down at 04:00 runs the pass when it comes back the same day; moving the time
later after today's pass does not run it twice; a user who gains their first eligible series at 15:00 gets a pass at the
next tick that day.

### The `syosetu_nightly` job (`api/syosetu_nightly.py`, general worker)

Registered with `@handler("syosetu_nightly")`. For each of the job owner's eligible series, read at run time (ordered by
id), with `ctx.progress(i, n, title)` between series (so Cancel stops between series):

1. Skip it (counted as `busy`) when `fetch_active(db, user_id, series_id)`.
2. Read the index with `scrape.fetch_series_index(c, url, delay=syosetu_delay_seconds)` through the job's `http_client`
   seam. `fetch_series_index` gains an opt-in `delay: float = 0.0`: when positive, `throttle(delay)` runs before every
   index page in `_walk_index` (a long series has one page per 100 chapters). Every other caller keeps `delay=0`, so the
   fetch job and the routers read as today. (Designer's call: the index is read unthrottled today.) On `ScrapeError` / `ValueError`, set `syosetu_check_error` to the message, add the series to
   `failed`, and go on to the next series.
3. On success: refresh `syosetu_updated_at` (`scrape.latest_post`), `syosetu_headings` (`heading_sequence`, as the fetch
   job does), `syosetu_checked_at = now`, `syosetu_check_error = ''`.
4. If the mark is NULL, seed it (see The mark) and stop here for this series. Otherwise `nums` = index chapter numbers
   above `max(mark, highest series_order)` (`highest` is 0 for a series with no chapters). If non-empty, enqueue `syosetu_fetch` with `{"series_id", "nums"}` and `total=len(nums)`, exactly as
   `POST /series/{id}/syosetu/fetch` does.

The job's writes commit with the job (they are in `ctx.db`). Result:

```json
{"checked": 12, "busy": 1,
 "queued": [{"series_id": 3, "title": "…", "chapters": 4, "job_id": 88}],
 "failed": [{"series_id": 7, "title": "…", "message": "…"}]}
```

Its final message reads like "12 series checked, 2 with new chapters, 1 failed".

Fetch jobs it queues are ordinary jobs, claimed after it by the single worker in id order. A large backlog holds the worker
for a while (about 5 s per chapter at the default delay); that is accepted, as it happens at night.

### SPA

- Jobs page / running-jobs button (`web/src/jobs/RunningJobs.tsx`): label `syosetu_nightly` "Nightly syosetu check" and
  render its result: series with new chapters (linking to the series), and failures with their messages.
- Series Settings tab (`web/src/library/SeriesSettings.tsx`), only when the series has a `syosetu_url`: a checkbox "Fetch
  new chapters nightly" (`syosetu_nightly`), a line "Last checked <date>" from `syosetu_checked_at`, and, when
  `syosetu_check_error` is non-blank, "Nightly check failed: <message>". When the series is not Ongoing or is archived, a
  hint says it is skipped for that reason.
- `npm run gen:api` after the schema change.

### Backup and restore

The new columns ride the table CSVs; the new settings are `server_settings` rows. Nothing to add. While a restore runs the
tick and the worker claim nothing, as today.

### Tests

- `tick`: disabled; before the time; at/after the time queues one job per user with eligible series; no second job for the
  same local day (including after a failed or cancelled one); the zone decides the day; ineligible series (not ongoing,
  archived, no URL, flag off) queue nothing.
- Handler with a `MockTransport` index: date and checked-at refreshed, error cleared; `nums` strictly above
  `max(mark, highest series_order)` (a gap below is not fetched; chapters deleted from the top are not fetched again);
  a NULL mark is seeded from the index and nothing is queued; a series created with mark 0 and only chapter 1 gets
  the rest; index pages are throttled when `delay` is set; nothing new queues nothing;
  busy series skipped; one series' index failure records its error and the others still run; cancel between series.
- Index reads in `fetch_job` and `_read_index` set `syosetu_checked_at` and clear the error.
- The fetch job raises a non-NULL mark to its highest added chapter and leaves a NULL mark alone.
- Migration: existing rows NULL, a new `Series` row 0.
- `server_settings` validation of the three keys.
